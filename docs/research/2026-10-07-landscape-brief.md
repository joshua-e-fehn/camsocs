# Cambridge societies and clubs database: landscape brief

This brief merges 8 fact-finding reports:
- **The original six:** universe, events, competitors, legal, sports and contacts.
- **The college-tier follow-up.**
- **The competitors follow-up,** which covers SocTrackr, other live tools and Cambridge SU's own position.

All checks were made on **2026-10-07** unless a line gives another date.

**How to read the tags**
- **No tag:** checked on the cited primary page or file, or counted from the reporter's own parse or crawl, on 2026-10-07.
- **[S]:** secondary source or search snippet only.
- **[INF]:** a reporter's inference or analysis. It is not a sourced fact, and the legal analysis is not legal advice.
- **[UNVERIFIED]:** could not be confirmed.
- Where reports disagree, §10 gives both versions and says which source carries more weight.
- **Personal identifiers** found during research (officer names, CRSids, personal emails) are counted, not copied. Names appear only for public office-holders, such as Cambridge SU sabbatical officers.

### 1.5 Edge cases the scope must decide on

- **Independent charities that are also registered societies.** The Cambridge Union appears on both the Proctors' and SU lists. Wikipedia says it is an independent charity open to ARU students and the public [S].
- **Student companies, festivals and services:**
  - Varsity Publications Ltd is on both the fair list and the Proctors' list.
  - Watersprite and Cambridge Nightline are on the fair list.
  - A society needs Finance Committee permission to trade on University premises and may be required to become a limited company [https://www.proctors.cam.ac.uk/files/jp_notes_of_guidance_for_societies_2024.pdf].
- **Intercollegiate groups:** CICCU, Addenbrooke's Intercollegiate Boat Club, the Intercollegiate Debating Society, and the clinical-school and vet-school clubs.
- **Clubs shared between colleges:** about 30 clubs appear on 2–5 college lists each (college follow-up). Examples:
  - Pirton RFC (Pembroke–Girton).
  - Newbroke (Newnham–Pembroke).
  - All Greys.
  - Penguins Ultimate (5 colleges).
  - Clare Hall shares a football team with Clare [https://www.clarehall.cam.ac.uk/social/].
- **University-level groups on college lists:** about 15–25 college-list entries are really university-level groups or events, such as RAG, Student Minds, May Balls and the Varsity Ski Trip (college follow-up).
- **College-run vs student-run:** Wolfson lists about 3 college-run societies (Science, Humanities, Lunchtime Seminars) alongside the student association's groups [https://www.wolfson.cam.ac.uk/current-students/societies].
- **JCR vs MCR lists:** separate lists often overlap heavily. For example, Pembroke's JPC lists 35 groups and its Graduate Parlour 36, mostly the same clubs. Colleges typically have 3–8 MCR-only groups (college follow-up).
- **Dormant entries:** Darwin (16 of 46) and Magdalene (15 of 47) list dormant groups separately. Most other lists do not (§1.6).
- **Oxbridge-joint groups and events:**
  - The Varsity Trip is on the fair list.
  - Varsity matches.
  - The Oxbridge AI Challenge, run by CUE [S] [https://ie.cam.ac.uk/support_article/cambridge-university-entrepreneurs-cue].
- **Groups that cross universities:** ARU Boat Club rows in CUCBC [https://www.cucbc.org/clubs].
- **Fair stalls that are not societies:**
  - The Careers Service, Estates, Kettle's Yard, the Language Centre, Cambridge Zero, Cambridge Enterprise, the City Council and CUOTC.
  - About 14 stalls carry a "University Department" label.
  - Brand Avenue lists 19 brands "plus more" [https://www.cambridgesu.co.uk/freshers/freshersfair/brandavenue/].
- **One-off events:** the 2020 SU rules excluded one-off events such as May Balls and productions [https://www.cambridgesu.co.uk/pageassets/opportunities/start-a-society/Registration-of-Cambridge-SU-Student-Clubs-and-Societies.pdf].

### 1.6 College tier in detail (college follow-up)

**Coverage:** 29 of 31 colleges have a count.
- **Not counted:** Lucy Cavendish, whose list is members-only (403), and Peterhouse, whose page returned 403.
- **Gonville & Caius:** covered only by the college's own claim, because its JCR site blocks all crawlers in robots.txt.

**Method:**
- Raw HTML was fetched with Python, and robots.txt was checked before every fetch.
- Contacts were classified by pattern and counted only.
- Wolfson's student site resets connections, so its data comes from a web.archive.org copy dated **2025-08-13**.
- The session's web-search limit (200 searches) ran out partway through. The last colleges were found by following links from their official sites.

**Column key:**
- **S/NS:** sports / non-sports.
- **Contacts:** counts only.
- **CRSid:** a personal CRSid, shown as a mailto link or plain text.
- **role:** a role address on a college, JCR or club domain.
- **Named:** whether officer names are shown.

#### Revised college-tier total [INF]

**Observed counts**
- The 24 undergraduate colleges sum to **791** listed groups. The mean is 33 and the median 35. The range runs from 17 (Murray Edwards' official page) to 57 (Jesus JCSU).
- **Lists kept by student unions undercount less than college pages.**
  - Union lists (Jesus, Girton, Trinity Hall, Queens', Christ's, Fitzwilliam, Emmanuel, Robinson, Corpus, Clare, Pembroke) cluster around **35–57, median about 40**.
  - College marketing pages show only 17–25 groups.
- The 5 graduate and mature colleges sum to **126** active groups (9–34 each), plus 16 dormant at Darwin.

**Undergraduate colleges (26)**
- 35–45 student-union groups each, plus 3–8 groups that exist only at the MCR. The Jesus MCR has about 7 that are not on the JCSU list, Corpus has 2 MCR teams, and the Pembroke Graduate Parlour has about 5 extra.
- That gives roughly **990–1,380 raw entries**.
- Subtract about 40–60 clubs that appear on several college lists (about 30 shared clubs found, each on 2–5 lists).
- Subtract about 15–25 entries that are really university-level groups or events.
- Subtract about 5–15% for stale lists (Catz, Homerton, Corpus 2023, St John's 2023-24).
- **Result: about 780–1,100.**

**Graduate and mature colleges (5):** about **125–150** active groups.

**Boat clubs (31):** these already appear on almost every list, so nothing was added for them.

**College football:** see §1.1. Second and third XIs add about **15–30** team records.

## 5. Legal and governance constraints, and the minimum compliant setup

### 5.1 Regulator and law changes in force now
- The ICO became the **Information Commission** on 30 Sep 2026; its powers are unchanged [https://www.gov.uk/government/news/information-commission-succeeds-the-ico-as-uks-data-protection-regulator].
- Most of the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 has been in force since 5 Feb 2026. Section 103 (complaints) followed on 19 Jun 2026 [https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2026/82].

### 5.2 Personal data
- **Role addresses:**
  - A generic address such as info@ "would usually be considered marketing to a corporate subscriber" (ICO) [https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/advice-for-small-organisations/direct-marketing-and-data-protection/marketing-and-data-protection-in-detail/].
  - If you don't know the person's name, you are not processing personal data [S].
  - PECR still applies "even if you only hold a generic or role-based address" [https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/guidance-on-direct-marketing-using-electronic-mail/key-concepts-for-direct-marketing-using-electronic-mail/].
  - A role address becomes personal data once the database also stores who holds the role [INF].
- **Named officers** are personal data.
  - Inferred special category data counts as special category data "regardless of how confident you are" [https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/lawful-basis/special-category-data/what-is-special-category-data/].
  - The relevant exceptions are Art 9(2)(d), for not-for-profit bodies and only if the data is not disclosed outside the body, and Art 9(2)(e), for data the person has manifestly made public [https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2016/679/article/9].
  - Naming the officers of faith, political, LGBTQ+ or ethnicity societies can therefore reveal special category data [INF].
- **College-tier contacts are mostly personal** (college follow-up, §7.1):
  - The college lists show about 630 personal CRSid identifiers, against about 120 role addresses and about 18 webmail addresses.
  - About 46% of the roughly 930 observed groups show a personal CRSid, usually alongside a named officer.
  - Stable role mailboxes exist mainly at Queens' and Jesus MCR.
  - Bulk-storing these CRSids means processing personal data tied to named students [INF].
- **Public social pages:**
  - Data being public does not by itself allow its use for direct marketing [https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/direct-marketing-guidance/collect-information-and-generate-leads/].
  - A 2023 joint statement by the ICO and 11 other regulators on scraping, with a 2024 follow-up, says the same [S].
- **Lawful basis:**
  - Legitimate interests requires a three-part test, and the right to object applies [https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/lawful-basis/legitimate-interests/what-is-the-legitimate-interests-basis/].
  - The new "recognised legitimate interests" (Art 6(1)(ea)) does not cover a directory or CRM. Art 6(11) gives direct marketing as an example of a possible legitimate interest [https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2025/18/section/70].
  - Legitimate interests (Art 6(1)(f)), with a written assessment, is the plausible basis [INF].
- **Telling people (Art 14):**
  - Within one month at most, or at first contact, or at the first disclosure to someone else, whichever comes first.
  - The notice must name the source, including whether it was "publicly accessible", and since 19 Jun 2026 the right to complain to the controller [https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2016/679/article/14].
  - The "disproportionate effort" exception (now Art 14(5)(e)) requires protective measures, including making the information public [https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2025/18/section/77]. The ICO says not to rely on it routinely.
  - Precedent: *ICO v Experian* [2024] UKUT 105 (AAC) [https://www.judiciary.uk/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/Information-Comissioner-v-Experian-Press-Summary.pdf].
- **DPIA:**
  - The ICO lists "invisible processing" (list brokering, data aggregation, re-use of public data) and "data matching" as likely high risk.
  - A DPIA is required when either is combined with another high-risk criterion [https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/accountability-and-governance/data-protection-impact-assessments-dpias/examples-of-processing-likely-to-result-in-high-risk/].
- **Complaints, objection and records:**
  - Since 19 Jun 2026 there must be an easy complaints route, with acknowledgement within 30 days (DPA 2018 s.164A) [https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2025/18/section/103].
  - The right to object to direct marketing is absolute (Art 21) [https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2016/679/article/21].
  - A directory that is maintained continuously is "not occasional", so Art 30 records are needed [https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2016/679/article/30].
- **Data protection fee:**

  | Tier | Who | Fee |
  |---|---|---|
  | 1 | Turnover ≤ £632k, ≤ 10 staff, or a charity | £52 (£47 by direct debit) |
  | 2 | Turnover ≤ £36m or ≤ 250 staff | £78 |
  | 3 | Everyone else | £3,763 |

  - Source: [https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2018/480/regulation/3].
  - The exemptions for not-for-profit members' processing and for an organisation's own marketing do not cover publishing third-party contacts or a brokering CRM, so expect Tier 1 [INF] [https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2018/480/schedule].
  - Fixed penalties for not paying start at £400 [https://ico.org.uk/about-the-ico/our-information/fixed-penalties-for-failure-to-pay-the-data-protection-charge/].
  - The University says most student societies themselves are exempt [https://www.information-compliance.admin.cam.ac.uk/data-protection/guidance/societies].

### 5.3 PECR (unsolicited email)
- **Who counts as what:**
  - An "individual" subscriber includes "an unincorporated body of such individuals" [https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2003/2426/regulation/2].
  - Most Cambridge societies are unincorporated members' clubs (JP notes).
  - The University is a common-law corporation and exempt charity [https://www.legal.admin.cam.ac.uk/faqs/governance].
  - Cambridge SU is charity no. 1189287 with Articles of Association [https://register-of-charities.charitycommission.gov.uk/en/charity-search/-/charity-details/5158360].
- **Rules:**
  - Reg 22: marketing email to individual subscribers needs prior consent, apart from the soft opt-in and the new charity soft opt-in [https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2003/2426/regulation/22].
  - Reg 23: never hide the sender's identity, and always give a valid opt-out [https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2003/2426/regulation/23].
  - The ICO says "If you are unsure… treat the details as belonging to an individual subscriber" [https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/business-to-business-marketing/].
- **Fines:** PECR enforcement under s.115 has been in force since 5 Feb 2026. The maximum is reported as £17.5m or 4% of global turnover [S] [https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2025/06/the-data-use-and-access-act-pecr-reform-rules-relating-to-electronic-marketing-and-cookies-in-the-uk].
- **University network:** CUDN rule 8.3 bans unsolicited commercial email over the University network [https://help.uis.cam.ac.uk/node/1017]. Bulk external email risks blacklisting [https://help.uis.cam.ac.uk/node/1010].
- **Analysis [INF]:**
  - Society Gmail addresses are very likely individual subscribers.
  - `@cam.ac.uk` and company addresses are corporate subscribers. A personal CRSid at `@cam.ac.uk` still belongs to a named individual and is personal data (§5.2).
  - Treat `@srcf.net` and `lists.cam.ac.uk` addresses as individual.
  - The platform can be liable as an "instigator" when it sends on a sponsor's behalf.

### 5.4 Scraping, platform terms and database right
- **Social platforms:**
  - Instagram's terms ban automated collection without permission [S] [https://help.instagram.com/581066165581870].
  - Meta's Automated Data Collection Terms (7 Oct 2024) say the same [S] [https://www.facebook.com/legal/automated_data_collection_terms].
  - LinkedIn's User Agreement (3 Nov 2025) bans scraping [https://www.linkedin.com/legal/user-agreement].
- **Database right:**
  - It exists where there has been "substantial investment" (reg 13).
  - It is infringed by extracting a substantial part, including by "repeated and systematic extraction" of small parts (reg 16).
  - It lasts 15 years and renews with substantial changes (reg 17).
  - Fair dealing covers only non-commercial teaching or research (reg 20).
  - [https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/1997/3032/regulation/16]
- **Contract:** *Ryanair v PR Aviation* held that site terms can restrict scraping even of databases with no IP protection [S] [https://gowlingwlg.com/en/insights-resources/articles/2015/ryanair-flying-high-at-the-cjeu].
- **cam.ac.uk terms:** no commercial use without a licence.
- **Computer Misuse Act s.1:** relevant to any Raven-gated content [https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1990/18/section/1]. Members-only college lists (Homerton, Lucy Cavendish) fall into this category.
- **AI-agent blocks:** talks.cam [https://talks.cam.ac.uk/robots.txt] and tbc.xyz [https://tbc.xyz/robots.txt] block Claude agents by name.
- **Obfuscation and full blocks on college sites:**
  - Queens' JCR hides 42 role addresses behind Cloudflare email protection [https://qjcr.org.uk/resources/clubs-societies].
  - Wolfson WCSA hides emails behind a script.
  - The Caius JCR server blocks all crawlers in robots.txt.
  - Decoding or getting around these goes against a clear signal from the site owner [INF].
- **Precedent, SocTrackr:**
  - It scrapes Instagram through Apify and publishes no privacy notice, terms or opt-out.
  - It uses the University coat of arms as its share image [https://github.com/AryaaSk/soctrackr] [https://www.soctrackr.com/sources].
  - It shows in practice the exposures described in §5.2–§5.5 [INF].

### 5.5 University and SU rules
- **Proctors' registration:**
  - Voluntary. It gives use of the University name and branding [https://www.proctors.cam.ac.uk/files/PN_2025%E2%80%932026_5_Clubs_and_Societies.pdf].
  - Societies are "bodies independent of the University".
  - The Proctors' role is "disciplinary – not a regulatory or supervisory role" (JP notes).
- **Member data (JP notes):**
  - §17: passing members' details to "external sponsors, or even to other clubs… you may be breaking the law".
  - §18: a warning about agencies that "harvest membership contact details in return for sponsorship".
- **Brand:**
  - Students may not use the logo for promotion, including on websites.
  - Unregistered groups may not use the name or logo without permission.
  - The brand is never used to imply endorsement [https://www.cam.ac.uk/brand-resources/logo/who-can-use].
  - The name and arms are trade-marked [https://www.cam.ac.uk/brand-resources/trademark-and-licensing].
- **SU directory:** listing is opt-in. Societies agree their data can be published by the SU. They re-register every year or are archived, and must report contact changes within a week.
- **SU sponsorship handling:** societies invoice sponsors through the SU finance system [https://www.cambridgesu.co.uk/pageassets/opportunities/support/resources/Society-Finance-Guide.docx.pdf]. No SU sponsorship-brokering service was found.
- **SU Student Council ethics policies** [https://www.cambridgesu.co.uk/makeachange/studentcouncil/policy/]:
  - "Support Fossil Free Careers and Advocate for an Ethical Careers Policy" calls for "removal of oil, gas and coal mining companies from careers advertising". It was renewed 28/10/2024 and expires in 2027.
  - "Support Ongoing Climate Campaigns … Fossil Fuel Industry", which absorbed "Boycott Barclays", passed 6/11/23 and expires in 2026.
  - A campaign to end the University's partnerships with organisations involved in "genocide, occupation, surveillance and weapons manufacture" was passed by referendum on 30/10/2025 and expires in 2028.
  - None of these explicitly binds society sponsorship. This is read from the wording only [INF].
- **College rules:** Selwyn's rules require each registered society to send the Dean its committee contact details by 15 September every year [https://www.sel.cam.ac.uk/sites/default/files/assets/current/UGHandbook/Selwyn-student-clubs-and-societies.docx]. The file is dated 2019, so whether the rule is current is [UNVERIFIED]. Whether other colleges have similar rules was not checked.
- **Other precedents:**
  - Camdram lets society admins manage their data and removes accounts dormant for two years [https://www.camdram.net/privacy].
  - Google Business Profile ownership claims give the current owner 3 days to respond [S].

### 7.5 Proposed staleness policy (contacts report) [INF]
- **Society-level channels:** weekly automated link checks, plus full re-verification each year from mid-September to mid-October.
- **Named contacts:**
  - Expire on 30 June of the academic year they were captured, and are flagged "needs reconfirm" from 1 July.
  - Termly posts expire at the end of term.
  - Senior Treasurers are reconfirmed every year.
- **Activity status:**
  - Active: at least 2 fresh signals in the last term.
  - Dormant: no fresh signal in 12 months.
  - Archived: removed from the SU or Proctors' list.
  - Never count Sympa or SRCF existence alone.
- **College tier:** re-verify in Michaelmas, after college registration deadlines (e.g. Selwyn's 15 September) and once JCR/MCR lists refresh.
- **Validation:**
  - Check email syntax and MX.
  - Flag `@cusu` addresses and Mailman links as legacy, and catch typo domains.
  - Canonicalise `srcf.ucam.org` and `*.soc.ucam.org` URLs.
  - Flag LinkedIn links that point to a global parent organisation.

## 9. Facts that bear directly on decisions the owner must make

- **Scope: which universe?**
  - University-level: about 650–700 entities.
  - **College tier: about 900–1,250 active entities (central about 1,050), or about 1,050–1,500 with sub-teams.**
    - That is about 1.5× the University tier, and about 98% of it is missing from the SU directory.
    - Leaving colleges out of the MVP means leaving out most groups [INF].
  - All tiers combined: about 1,550–1,950 [INF].
  - Theatre: 97 Camdram companies, including defunct and one-off ones.
  - ARU: 232 entries, 170 inactive.
  - Also to decide:
    - One-off events (the SU excluded May Balls).
    - Military units.
    - Independent bodies (the Union, Varsity Publications Ltd).
    - University departments as fair stalls.
    - College-run vs student-run societies.
    - University-level groups that appear on college lists.
- **Entity model:**
  - Clubs have several teams.
  - College teams and clubs often span colleges (about 30 shared clubs), and some entrants are not colleges.
  - Organisations hold several registrations that lapse independently.
  - JCR and MCR lists overlap.
  - So: a hierarchy, team child records, a many-to-many club ↔ college link, a college_body field and separate registration flags.
- **Seeding: scrape or ask?**
  - SU robots.txt does not block `/organisation/`.
  - But SU societies consented to publication *by the SU* only, no reuse licence was found, database right covers "repeated and systematic extraction", and the cam.ac.uk terms forbid commercial use without a licence.
  - **College tier:**
    - About 24 of 31 colleges have public lists, across about 15 different site templates, so each needs its own parser.
    - Girton's data sits in a JS bundle, Newnham's is a PDF, and Wolfson's needs an archived copy.
    - Homerton, Lucy Cavendish, Caius and Peterhouse are gated or blocked.
  - **Partner route:** colleges hold registers (Selwyn: committee contacts to the Dean by 15 September). Partnering with Deans and JCR/MCR sports & societies officers would be more authoritative than scraping [INF].
  - So: decide whether to seek data-sharing agreements with Cambridge SU, the Proctors' Office, the Sports Service and the colleges or common rooms before building on their lists.
  - SocTrackr's 155-handle seed list is useful, but its code has no licence, so reuse needs the author's permission.
- **Taxonomy:** reuse the SU's 7 categories, which the Fair already uses, and add:
  - tags or collections;
  - a department filter (as Edinburgh does);
  - a college filter;
  - a JCR/MCR-funded flag;
  - the sport attributes from the guide.
- **Primary dedup key:**
  - At University level, Instagram covers 87% of SU societies and 96% of sports clubs, so use it alongside the SU grouping-id.
  - At college level, Instagram links are much sparser on the lists (e.g. Girton 15 of 51). Dedup there needs college plus normalised name [INF].
- **Public vs internal fields:**
  - Officer names for faith, political, LGBTQ+ or ethnicity societies can reveal special category data.
  - Sympa info pages, Camdram descriptions and JCR pages already publish names; republishing them is still processing.
  - Personal CRSids make up roughly three-quarters to four-fifths of college-tier contact identifiers (§10).
  - So: role addresses in public, people only in the internal CRM, and common-room officers as the routing point for college groups.
- **The claim flow is a compliance requirement, not just a feature:**
  - Art 14 requires notice within one month.
  - The disproportionate-effort exception is weak here.
  - The SU already requires annual re-registration and updates within a week.
  - Committee turnover makes a claim and re-confirm loop the main way to keep data fresh.
  - It is also the clearest point of difference from SocTrackr, which never asked societies [INF].
- **Events strategy:**
  - Open structured sources cover only theatre, a few talk lists, Playwaze sport and (for innovation events) camevents.
  - Most societies are on Instagram, which cannot be scraped (Meta terms).
    - SocTrackr proved the volume (1,858 events from 138 accounts) but did it through Apify scraping, and it went dormant.
  - talks.cam and tbc.xyz block AI agents by name in robots.txt, which matters if the pipeline uses agents such as Claude.
  - **The live competitor for a feed is .tbc (ex-Youni),** a funded company aggregating "socials, ticketing sites and the web".
  - So: rely on societies opting in, by giving ICS or Luma feeds, connecting their own Instagram professional account, or uploading termcards. Ingest open feeds (Camdram, talks.cam within its rules, Playwaze, the What's On ICS) rather than rebuilding them. Do not compete on the feed alone [INF].
- **Refresh cadence:**
  - Re-sync after the SU handover (end of August), after college registration deadlines and the Freshers' Fair (September–October), and after the Proctors' deadline (31 December).
  - Playwaze league IDs roll over each season; the 2026-27 ones were not live on 2026-10-07.
  - The Sport guide is republished each September.
- **Brokering design:**
  - Society Gmail addresses are probably PECR "individual" subscribers that need consent [INF].
  - The Junior Proctor warns against sharing members' details and against agencies that "harvest" contacts.
  - CUDN rule 8.3 bans bulk commercial email over University systems.
  - The Careers Service uses an opt-in form and does not pass on contacts.
  - So: opt-in flags, plus relayed introductions rather than exported addresses.
  - Kaampus's model of uploading member emails conflicts with JP notes §17.
- **Sponsor value:**
  - No platform offers member counts, audience profile, past sponsors or package pricing per society.
  - Huzzle's pivot leaves this open. .tbc/Youni planned a "connection fee" brokering model in 2025, but whether it runs one today is [UNVERIFIED].
  - The data must be gathered from societies themselves.
  - UCL shows rules to plan for: banned sectors, SU-signed contracts, terms of one committee year.
  - The CRM needs ethics filters that mirror SU Student Council policy (fossil fuels; weapons and surveillance).
- **Position relative to the SU:**
  - The SU already sells reach to "over 24,000 students" through native, its exclusive media partner.
  - The MSL platform already supports events, tickets and elections.
  - No new SU directory, app or events platform appears in the 2026-27 officers' manifestos, and the SU offers no sponsorship brokering.
  - So: the SU looks like a data source and partner rather than a competitor [INF]. The VP Student Communities & Societies (Wilkinson) is the natural contact. Any commercial tie-up must avoid colliding with native's exclusive media deal.
- **Name and branding:**
  - No crest; no "University of Cambridge" or "Cambridge University" in the product name; add a disclaimer.
  - Whether "Cambridge" alone is protected is [UNVERIFIED].
  - Registering as a University society is one route to using the name.
- **Compliance budget:** Tier 1 fee (£52), a legitimate interests assessment, a DPIA (aggregation and data matching are listed as likely high risk), a complaints form with 30-day acknowledgement, and Art 30 records.
