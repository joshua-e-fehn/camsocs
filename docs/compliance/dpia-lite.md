## 1. Screening: is a DPIA required?

The ICO lists "invisible processing" (data aggregation, re-use of public data) and "data matching" as likely high risk, needing a DPIA when combined with another criterion (landscape brief §5.2).

| Criterion | Public directory (A) | Broker CRM (B) |
|---|---|---|
| Invisible processing (aggregating public sources without telling people) | Yes, until the Art 14 notice goes out (J7, by 9 Nov 2026) | Yes |
| Data matching across sources | Organisations only, not people | Names matched to organisations |
| Large scale | ~1,500 organisations; few personal data items each | Hundreds of named officers at most |
| Special category data | No (faith / political / LGBTQ+ societies listed, members not) | Risk: naming the officers of such societies can reveal affiliation |
| Innovative technology | LLM extraction via a third-party provider | Same |
| Vulnerable people | No (adult students) | No |

**Outcome:** A — a full DPIA is not strictly required but this lite assessment is kept because of aggregation and the LLM. B — required; this document is its first version and must be extended before any outreach from the CRM.

**Site analytics and monitoring (P11, P12), screened 2026-11-01:** no special category data, no profiling, no identification of anyone (no `identify()`, no person profiles, the visitor's IP never forwarded to PostHog), no data matching, aggregate purpose, a processor with EU hosting (its DPA still to be signed, sign-off below). Not likely high risk, so no full DPIA; the risks are R-15 and R-16 below.

## 3. Risks and measures

Likelihood (L) and severity (S): low / medium / high. "Residual" is after the measures.

| # | Risk | L | S | Measures (in place unless marked **todo**) | Residual |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| R-1 | A personal CRSid address or a named officer appears on the public site | M | M | Public projection removes `personal_crsid` emails and personal role emails (`convex/lib/projections.ts`, tested in `convex/public.test.ts`); `people` is broker-only; pipeline never writes names into public fields (AGENTS.md data honesty rules). **D1 redaction:** names of private individuals in descriptions, notes, event and session titles, venues and price notes are replaced by roles before any claim is posted (`extract/redact.py`: LLM pass with deterministic checks; a rule fallback that withholds a text it cannot make safe; a title it cannot make safe keeps its event out; known committee names always removed, and never published as speakers; profile links and URLs spelling a name cut). Room-booking calendar entries are not events, and feed text keeps only the organisation's own addresses (2026-10-10, after 21 Newnham MCR bookings with bookers' names, webmail addresses and CRSids were found published on dev) | Low |
| R-2 | Inaccurate data presented as fact (stale contacts, wrong officers) | H | L | Per-fact status / source / date; LLM values without a verified quote shown as `inferred`; conflicts shown with both readings; annual refresh; correction form | Low |
| R-3 | Societies do not know they are listed (invisible processing) | H | M | Art 14 notice to every listed role address by 9 Nov 2026 (J7; Art 14(3)(a)) with claim / correct / remove links and the right to object; public privacy notice; About page | Low once J7 is done |
| R-4 | LLM provider processes personal data in list mail and pages (US transfer; consumer-plan terms, possible use for training) | M | M | Only page text and list-mail text needed for extraction are sent; talks.cam never sent; list mail is cleaned deterministically before extraction — quoted replies, forwarded headers, signatures and list footers cut out, addresses and phone numbers redacted (`pipeline/.../extract/mail_text.py`, tested on fixtures); **todo:** turn off model-training on the ChatGPT account used by cliproxy; record the transfer basis (record-of-processing, transfers) | Medium until the todos are done |
| R-5 | Mailing-list content leaks to the public | L | M | Events from mail default to `cam_only`; `cam_only` needs a verified `@cam.ac.uk` session; a mailing-list event has no public `sourceUrl`; raw mail never in a public query; bodies deleted 30 days after processing, rows after 90 (automated, `retention.md` R4) | Low |
| R-6 | Broker CRM used for unsolicited marketing (PECR reg 22) | M | M | PECR subscriber type per address, default individual; no marketing without consent; suppression list; identify sender and give an opt-out; introductions relay, no export (LIA §B) | Low |
| R-7 | Officers of faith / political / identity societies revealed (Art 9) | L | H | No names in public fields; no inference about people; broker rows private and time-boxed | Low |
| R-8 | A removed listing is re-created by the next harvest | M | M | Suppression list (`suppressions`; listing, field, address hash or person): enforced when a removal is accepted, filtered by the pipeline before every load, refused by every ingestion route, stripped from the public projection (`convex/lib/suppressions.ts`, `pipeline/.../suppressions.py`, tested on both sides) | Low |
| R-9 | Unauthorised access to broker data | L | H | `requireBrokerUser` on every broker function, `BROKER_EMAILS` allowlist, verified email only; secrets out of Git; Convex and Vercel access protected by Joshua's accounts (2FA **todo:** confirm on all four providers) | Low |
| R-10 | Scraping beyond what sites allow (terms, robots, Computer Misuse Act for gated pages) | M | M | **Decision D3 (2026-10-08):** society websites, Linktree pages, Google Calendar ICS feeds societies link, SU `/asset/` files linked from a profile and college / JCR / MCR society lists are read even where robots.txt or an AI opt-out disallows; the operator's reasoning is that societies publish these pages to be found. Limits: one policy (`sources/policy.py`) with the URL classes fixed and tested; never talks.cam or tbc.xyz; never non-society pages (their robots.txt holds); each override recorded on the `sources` row. **Decision D3a (2026-11-02):** the University's public events programme (What's On, Cambridge Festival, Open Cambridge) is a new source, read with the permission of University Information Services and the Office of External Affairs and Communications: only its events API and event images (`policy.RECORDED_PERMISSIONS`), recorded `robots_override:permission` / `ai_optout_override:permission`; the rest of that host keeps its robots.txt; the programme list's staff contacts are never stored; its event text reaches the model only for D1 redaction and D6 noise; withdrawing the permission is a runbook (docs/ops-vps.md). Unchanged: Crawl-delay and the minimum interval, one request per host, honest User-Agent, no Raven / members-only / 403 pages, no decoding of obfuscated emails, no Meta / LinkedIn scraping. A site owner who objects can use the request form; **todo:** a per-host opt-out list the policy checks | Low–medium |
| R-11 | Over-retention | M | L | `retention.md` schedule, automated for R3–R8, R10 and R11 (daily `retention-daily` and `talks-retention-daily` crons; `prune-cache` on the pipeline host); R12 by PostHog's plan retention (1 year, checked each July), R13 in the visitor's browser; R1 and R9 by hand | Low |
| R-12 | Unredacted originals (with names) kept in the internal claim log | M | L | `fieldClaims.valueRaw` is internal: no query returns it, public or broker; kept with the entity (`retention.md` R2), and cleared from a free-text claim as soon as a newer reading supersedes it (2026-10-10); claims set aside by a removal keep no value, original or quote | Low |
| R-13 | The production pipeline key, the cache and staged pages (which can hold names) on a shared host beside an LLM agent that browses the web (D5) | L | H | Not in the agent's container: its own user and systemd units (`ProtectSystem=strict`, `NoNewPrivileges`, empty capability set, `/docker` inaccessible, private and Tailscale address ranges denied), env files `0600`, a read-only deploy key, its own cliproxy key on loopback. The fetcher refuses any URL that resolves to a non-public address (the host's loopback services, Docker's bridge, link-local, CGNAT), at every redirect and again when it connects. Code root runs (the installer, the units, the alert and the harvest script) is installed root-owned from a reviewed commit fetched into root's own mirror, never from the checkout the harvest writes; re-running it keeps the operator's timer state. The alert runs as a user of its own (`DynamicUser`), not the harvest's, with a root-owned copy of only the three Slack values (`/etc/camsocs/alert.env`), and sends structured facts only (group, exit code, phase, commit, run key, failed stages' names, the log's path) — no log line, error text or scraped title reaches Slack. Logs and cache 90 days (R7), what quotes list mail 30 (R4), pruned after every run (`docs/ops-vps.md`; review 2026-10-10) | Low |
| R-14 | Speakers of every public University talk are republished by name (talks pillar, D7): a name stays findable after the speaker asked for it to go, or a listing the organiser took down lingers | M | M | Parser-only reading, speakers exactly as published, nothing inferred or aggregated about a person (LIA §C); a talk its source stops listing is `missing` and not shown; talks and details deleted 30 days after the talk (`talks-retention-daily`, `retention.md` R11); a removal or correction request becomes a name or contact suppression that `talks/batch` applies before storing and `ingest/talks:scrubStored` applies to stored talks (public queries scrub until it has run); robots.txt disallows `/talks` to AI crawlers, as talks.cam's does | Low |
| R-15 | Browser analytics without valid consent, or beyond what was consented (PECR reg 6; ICO consent rules) | M | M | posthog-js is not loaded at all before "Accept analytics" (no cookieless mode, no IP/UA hashing; the DUAA statistics exception is not relied on, record-of-processing "Storage and access"); Accept and Reject equal on the first layer; ignoring the banner is no; the banner names PostHog, the purpose, the `ph_*` storage and its 180-day lifetime; the choice (`camsocs-consent`) expires after 180 days; "Cookie settings" in every footer reopens it, and Reject stops capture at once and deletes every `ph_*` and `__ph_*` cookie and storage key (ENG-2). Never on `/dashboard` or `/sign-in`; never without a key or under `navigator.webdriver`. The ICO reads a withdrawal as a request for erasure, and PostHog is not told: the events already sent stay pseudonymous (the random browser identifier `$device_id` and the per-visit, per-tab and per-page-view identifiers `$session_id`, `$window_id`, `$pageview_id`, `$prev_pageview_id`, with browser, device and referrer properties), but withdrawal deletes the stored identifiers from the browser, so the controller can no longer identify whose events they are. Under UK GDPR Art 11(1)–(2) Arts 15–20 then apply only if the visitor supplies the browser identifier (`/privacy` says so), and the events go with R12 | Low |
| R-16 | Personal data or talks.cam content reaches PostHog: URL query strings (`/request?…`), page titles (talk pages put the talk title in `<title>`), element text, search text, exception messages; PostHog AI reading talk data; access from the US through PostHog, Inc. | M | M | `before_send` (`sanitizeEvent`, `webapp/lib/analytics-client.ts`, ENG-4) drops events on `/dashboard` and `/sign-in` and every SDK event but `$pageview` / `$pageleave`, strips query strings and fragments from every URL and path property, and deletes `title`, `$title`, `$el_text`, `$elements`, `$elements_chain`, the full user-agent string (`$raw_user_agent`, `$useragent`, `$user_agent`), campaign parameters, the search-engine keyword, `$viewport_*`, `$device` and `$prev_pageview_duration`, also as their `$initial_…` / `$session_entry_…` copies; at the source `save_referrer` and `save_campaign_params` are off, scroll properties and URL fragments are not captured, and `$geoip_disable: true` rides on every event, so no location is derived; custom events keep only allow-listed IDs, enums, numbers and booleans (`docs/analytics.md`); search events carry length and result count, never the query; autocapture, replay, heatmaps, surveys and flags off; no `identify()`. The `/relay` route handler (`lib/relay.ts`) forwards only `content-type`, `content-encoding` and `accept`: no cookies (so no Better Auth session or `convex_jwt`), no authorization, no user agent, no `x-forwarded-for`, so PostHog sees the site's server, never the visitor's IP or browser string (the project also discards IP data). Server events: fixed id, no headers, cookies or bodies. Harvester and LLM metrics carry no text (`$ai_input` and `$ai_output_choices` never sent; the talks stage never calls the LLM). EU Cloud; PostHog's DPA provides the IDTA and the DPF UK Extension (record-of-processing, transfers; signature an open item, sign-off). PostHog AI switched off at organisation level (confirmed by Joshua, 2026-11-01) | Low |
