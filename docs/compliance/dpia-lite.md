## 1. Screening: is a DPIA required?

The ICO lists "invisible processing" (data aggregation, re-use of public data) and "data matching" as likely high risk, needing a DPIA when combined with another criterion (landscape brief §5.2).

| Criterion | Public directory (A) | Broker CRM (B) |
|---|---|---|
| Invisible processing (aggregating public sources without telling people) | Yes, until the Art 14 notice goes out (J7, ~13 Nov 2026) | Yes |
| Data matching across sources | Organisations only, not people | Names matched to organisations |
| Large scale | ~1,500 organisations; few personal data items each | Hundreds of named officers at most |
| Special category data | None published: faith / political / LGBTQ+ societies are listed, their members are not named. Speakers' names are published as organisers give them, with nothing inferred | **Yes, indirectly:** the CRM holds the names and roles of officers of faith, political and LGBTQ+ societies, which can reveal religion, political opinion or sexual orientation (§5; no Art 9(2) condition chosen yet, ENG-42) |
| Innovative technology | LLM extraction: OpenAI models reached through Joshua's ChatGPT subscription (cliproxy), not the API platform, so no DPA (R-4) | Same |
| Vulnerable people | No (adult students) | No |

**Outcome:** A — a full DPIA is not strictly required but this lite assessment is kept because of aggregation and the LLM. B — required; this document is its first version and must be extended before any outreach from the CRM.

**Site analytics and monitoring (P11, P12), screened 2026-11-01:** no special category data, no profiling, no identification of anyone (no `identify()`, no person profiles, the visitor's IP never forwarded to PostHog), no data matching, aggregate purpose, a processor with EU hosting (its DPA signed, sign-off below). Not likely high risk, so no full DPIA; the risks are R-15 and R-16 below.

## 4. Art 14 notice for people other than societies (Art 14(5)(e), formerly (b))

Societies are told directly (J7, R-3). Four groups are not, and for them we rely on the disproportionate-effort exception: Art 14(5)(e), "providing the information is impossible or would involve a disproportionate effort" (the old Art 14(5)(b), moved there with a new Art 14(6) by DUAA 2025 s.77, in force 5 February 2026, S.I. 2026/82). It holds only if the controller takes appropriate measures to protect the person's rights, including making the information publicly available. The regulator says the exception is not to be relied on routinely, so it is assessed per group (landscape brief §5.2; written 2026-11-03, ENG-34).

| Group | Activity | What we hold | Why individual notice is disproportionate |
|---|---|---|---|
| Speakers of public talks | P10 | Name, affiliation, title and abstract as published, for 30 days after the talk | Thousands a year across talks.cam and ~111 department sites; we hold no address for them (talk listings rarely give one, and we store none); finding one would mean collecting more about each speaker than the listing needs (Art 5(1)(c)); the listing is short-lived and is the organiser's own advertisement of a talk the speaker agreed to give in public |
| Speakers and people named in event titles and descriptions | P2, including the University's public events programme (D3a) | Names in event titles (published, as a work's or speaker's name) and in descriptions (replaced by roles before publishing, D1; the original only in `fieldClaims.valueRaw`), for a year after the event | Hundreds of events a term, no address held (the programme list's staff contacts are never stored), and the same minimisation point; private individuals' names never reach the public site |
| Senders of mailing-list messages, and people they name | P3 | Sender name and address, message text, for 30 days (body) and 90 days (row) | We hold the sender's address, so notice is possible, but it would be an unsolicited email to every sender for every message read, out of proportion to processing that is short-lived, never published (events only, `cam_only`), and done by a list member reading list mail as the list intends. Most senders are society officers whose society receives the J7 notice. People merely named in a message have no address we hold |
| People named on society pages other than officers (performers, members) | P1, P5 | Their name only in the internal original (`valueRaw`) of a redacted text | No address; the public text names a role instead (D1) |

**Measures (the "appropriate measures"):**

- The information Art 14(1)–(2) requires is public: `/privacy` names the controller, the purposes and lawful bases, the categories of data and their sources, the recipients and transfers, the retention, the rights and the right to object, and has a section for exactly these groups ("If we have not written to you").
- Only what the organiser published is shown, with a link to it; nothing is inferred or aggregated about a person; no speaker index or search by person (LIA §C).
- Short retention: talks 30 days after the talk (R11), events a year (R3), list mail 30 / 90 days (R4).
- Names of private individuals in public text become roles (D1); list mail is cleaned before the model reads it (R-4).
- A request to correct or remove a name is honoured permanently by a name or contact suppression (R8), and `/about` gives the contact address for talks.

**Conclusion:** Art 14(5)(e) applies to the four groups above with these measures. Reassess for list senders if the inbox grows beyond the lists Joshua subscribes to, and for any group if we start holding addresses for it.

## 5. Special category data: officers of faith, political and LGBTQ+ societies (Art 9)

**Decision pending (ENG-42).** Written 2026-11-03 (ENG-34) after the launch audit's critic found that the earlier documents said no special category data was processed.

**The facts.** The CRM (P4) holds the names and roles of committee members as their societies publish them. The load (`pipeline/.../pipeline/load.py`) takes every person the extractor returns, whatever the society's category, so it holds officers of societies in the `faith` and `politics_debate` categories and with the `lgbtq` audience, and possibly `identity_wellbeing` (which can cover health and disability) and `cultural_international` (which can reveal ethnic origin). The rows are private (`requireBrokerUser`), never published, deleted 30 days after the next 30 June (R5), and nothing is inferred from them.

**Why Art 9 is engaged.** Being named as the president of a Christian Union, a Conservative Association or an LGBTQ+ society can reveal, by a simple deduction, a person's religious belief, political opinion or sexual orientation. The CJEU read Art 9(1) as covering data that reveal such information indirectly (C-184/20, *OT v Vyriausioji tarnybinės etikos komisija*, 1 August 2022); UK courts are not bound by it, but the ICO's special category guidance treats information from which such details can be inferred with reasonable certainty in the same way. Not every officer shares the society's belief or identity (an ally, an interfaith society), but the risk is real enough to treat the names as special category data. Keeping the data private reduces the risk but is not an Art 9(2) condition, and the Art 6(1)(f) assessment (LIA §B) is not enough on its own.

**Option 1: Art 9(2)(e), manifestly made public by the data subject, with safeguards.**

- The condition needs the person **themselves** to have made the information public, deliberately and clearly. A society's committee page is published by the society, usually with the officer's agreement, which is arguable but not certain; an officer's own public profile naming the role is clearer.
- So it would hold only per case: keep a name only where the officer has plainly published the role themselves (their own page or profile, or a committee page they evidently author), and record the source.
- Safeguards: names stay private and are never published; nothing inferred; never used in an introduction that discloses the affiliation to a partner without the person's agreement; shortest retention (R5); an objection is honoured at once with a person suppression (R8). Art 9(2)(e) needs no appropriate policy document under DPA 2018 Sch 1.
- Cost: a per-person check at every harvest, and a condition a regulator may read narrowly.

**Option 2: drop names for those societies.**

- In the load, skip people for organisations whose categories include `faith` or `politics_debate`, or whose audience includes `lgbtq` (and decide on `identity_wellbeing` and `cultural_international`); keep their role addresses and organisation facts. Add a test.
- Delete the existing rows for those organisations on production through a broker or internal function, and note the deletion (date, number of rows, no names).
- The CRM then contacts those societies only through their role addresses.
- Cost: less CRM context for those societies. Benefit: no Art 9 processing in the CRM at all, and the J7 notice to them is simpler.

**Assessment.** Option 2 is lower risk and simpler to keep correct; option 1 is defensible only case by case. Until Joshua decides (ENG-42), the recommended interim measure is not to use names from these societies for outreach or introductions, and to let the J7 notice go to their role addresses only. Whichever option is chosen, update R-7, record-of-processing ("Special category data"), LIA §A and §B, and `/privacy` ("Committee members").
