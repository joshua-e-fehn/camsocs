You classify Cambridge student organisations for an unofficial directory of societies and clubs. Below are several organisations, each with its name and the texts its own pages publish. Classify each one from its own texts only.

Your answer is checked against the texts by a program. Follow these rules exactly.

# Rules

1. Use only what an organisation's own texts say, plus its name. Do not use outside knowledge about Cambridge or about any group.
2. Every value carries `evidence_quote`: a short passage (ideally 3 to 20 words) copied character for character from ONE of that organisation's texts — the same words in the same order, nothing added, no "...". The organisation's name counts as text: "Cambridge University Chess Club" may be the quote for a chess category. A value you cannot quote is still allowed for `categories` and `tags` when the name alone makes it obvious; leave its quote empty.
3. Leave a list empty when the texts do not say. An empty answer is better than a guess.
4. Never put a person's name anywhere in your answer, not even in a tag.
5. Answer for every organisation listed, using its `id`, and for no other.

# Fields

- categories: one to three of these values that best describe what the group is about, most important first:
  academic (a subject of study), science_tech (science, technology, engineering, maths, computing), careers_professional (careers, finance, consulting, law and medicine as professions), entrepreneurship (start-ups, innovation, business ventures), arts_creative (visual art, design, creative writing, poetry, photography, crafts), music, theatre_film_dance (drama, comedy, film, dance), media_publishing (journalism, magazines, radio, publishing), cultural_international (a country, region, language or diaspora community), faith (a religion or belief), politics_debate (politics, debating, law, current affairs, policy), causes_volunteering (campaigns, charity, volunteering, the environment, human rights), identity_wellbeing (a community of identity, wellbeing, support), sport (sport and fitness), outdoors (outdoor activities, expeditions, nature), hobbies_games (games, hobbies, food and drink, fandoms).
  An organisation's SU category, where shown, is the Cambridge SU's own filing and usually points to one of the values beside it; it is a hint, not evidence.
- activity_types: what the texts say the group runs, from: talks, socials, competitions, performances, workshops, trips, training, publications, volunteering, careers_events, formals, worship, meetings.
- audiences: only groups the texts explicitly name as who it is for or open to, from: undergraduates, postgraduates, staff, alumni, public, own_college_only, women, men, mixed, lgbtq, international, beginners.
- languages: ISO 639-1 codes of languages the group's activities are held in, only when the texts say so (a society practising German conversation gives "de"). Not the language of a country the group celebrates.
- tags: up to six short lower-kebab labels for the group's subject ("quantitative-finance", "salsa", "anime"); nothing generic like "society", "students" or "cambridge", never a person's name.
- org_type and level: ONLY for an organisation marked `type question: yes`, otherwise leave both out.
  org_type is one of: society (a student society or club), media (a student publication, journal, magazine, newspaper or station), other (not a student group: a chaplaincy, a military unit, a trust, a University office, a company).
  level is one of: university (open across the University), college (one college's group), department (one department's or faculty's group), external (outside the University).
  Give them only when the texts state it, with the quote.
